## Main Bearing Bore Inspection and Repair Guidelines (T814)

This service parts topic provides main bearing bore inspection and repair guidelines for the main bearing caps when there is evidence of main bearing cap fretting. These guidelines are **only** for M11/ISM and N14 Series engines.

Main bearing cap fretting is attributed to the failure of the main bearing capscrews to provide sufficient clamp load for extreme duty cycles. Fretting at the block/main bearing cap interface results in an oval shape and an overall decrease in the main bearing bore size. This decrease reduces the oil film thickness, which can lead to spun main bearings.

**Reuse Guidelines**

The following procedure need **only** to be performed if main bearing cap fretting is discovered during a main bearing inspection or a failure analysis of a spun main bearing.

Remove main bearing caps (no more than two at a time) to inspect for indications of fretting.

On the M11/ISM engines, fretting typically occurs on the number 2 and number 6 main bearing caps; therefore, inspection of the number 2 and number 6 main bearing caps **only** is sufficient.

On the N14 engine, fretting typically occurs on the number 2, number 3, number 5, and number 6 main bearing caps; therefore, inspection of the number 2, number 3, number 5, and number 6 main bearing caps **only** is sufficient.

If fretting on any of the main bearing caps is occurring, the inspection procedure, detailed in this topic, **must** be performed. Before performing the bore inspection, determine if the bearings are reusable according to the guidelines in the appropriate troubleshooting and repair manual. Also determine if there is side-to-side press fit of the main cap in the block. If there is, proceed with the inspection; if there is **not** a side-to-side press fit, a service cap **must** be used. If a service cap is required, the inspection procedure is **not** required and the engine will need to be removed from the chassis and prepared for line boring.

- For clearance greater than or equal to 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \], reassemble the main caps according to the guidelines presented in this topic. On the M11/ISM engines, the Torque-Plus-Angle (70 ft-lb plus 120-degree) method **must** be used for all engines. On the N14 engine, use the torque specification in use at the time the engine was built.
- For clearance less then 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \] on any main bore (or without a press fit) the engine needs to be removed and prepared for line boring. (Line-boring guidelines are included in this topic.)

**Plastigage™**

Plastigage™ provides a fast and accurate method to check bearing clearances. Plastigage™ is a special extruded plastic threaad of a definite diameter with accurately controlled crush properties.

Plastigage™ is packaged in calibrated envelopes, 12 envelopes per box. These envelopes not only protect the plastic threads but also serve directly as scales to measure the bearing clearance. both sides of the envelope have a printed scale of graduations. One side is calibrated in inches, the other in millimeters. The numbers on the scale are the bearing clearances in thousandths of an inch or millimeter. When the width of a compressed section of Plastigage™ in a bearing or journal is compared with the appropriate-numbered graduation, the bearing clearance can be read directly from the scale.

Plastigage™ is available, from a local parts store, in four styles to cover different clearance ranges. Both Plastigage™ thread and its matching envelope have a distinctive color for each clearance range.

The PG-1 (green) style, clearance range of 0.025 to 0.076 mm \[ 0.001 to 0.003 in \], is required for this procedure.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> The shims push the crankshaft up against the adjoining upper bearings so an accurate clearance measurement can be made on the lower bearing.

Compare the width of the compressed Plastigage™ at its widest point to the braduations on the envelope. The number within the graduation on the envelope indicates the bearing clearance in thousandths of an inch or millimeter, depending on which side of the envelope is used.

If the compressed Plastigage™ is less than the graduation marked 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \] at its widest point the block **must** be pulled from service and either line-bored or replaced.

If the clearance is greater than 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \] (**must** also have a press fit), reassemble the main bearing caps with existing or new main bearings.

If the clearance is less than 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \] on any main bearing bore (or without a side-to-side main cap press fit), the engine **must** be removed and prepared for line boring. (The line-boring procedure can be found at the end of this topic.)

Repeat the above steps to check the clearance at the number 6 main bearing cap (M11/ISM Series engine **only**)

Repeat the above steps to check the clearance at the number 3, number 5, and number 6 main bearing caps (N14 Series engine **only**)

**M11/ISM Main Bearing Capscrew Torque Procedures**

The torque procedure for the main bearing capscrews has changed for the M11/ISM Series engines. A torque-plus-angle method **must** be used. This method results in a more consistent loading of the joint than the previous torque- **only** method.

The new, proper torque procedure is as follows:

![[mb2cvsa.png]]

**M11/ISM Torque Procedure**

Coat the capscrew threads and both sides of the washers with clean lubricating engine oil. Drain the excess oil from the capscrews before installing them in the cylinder block. This allows the highest possible preload from the input torque.

Tighten the main bearing capscrews on the M11/ISM Series engine in the alternating sequence shown in the illustration to the following torque values:

- Step 1 **Torque Value:** 95 N•m \[ 70 ft-lb \]
- Step 2 Loosen Completely
- Step 3 **Torque Value:** 95 N•m \[ 70 ft-lb \]
- Step 4 Rotate 120 degrees

**N14 Main Bearing Capscrew Torque Procedures**

Depending on engine build date, there are two different procedures. They are as follows:

For engines built prior to January 5, 1998 (and prior to engine serial number 11875596), use this procedure that includes use of the 3/4 inch diameter, grade 8 capscrew, Part Number 208346.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> Use the torque procedure in the N14 Shop Manual, Bulletin 3810487, to tighten the N14 main caps in engines that were made prior to January 5, 1998.

![[mb8cvoa.png]]

Coat the capscrew threads and both sides of the washers with clean lubricating engine oil. Drain the excess oil from the capscrews before installing them in the cylinder block. This allows the highest possible preload from the input torque.

Tighten the main bearing capscrews on the N14 Series engine in the alternating sequence shown in the illustration to the following torque values:

- Step 1 **Torque Value:** 122 N•m \[ 90 ft-lb \]
- Step 2 **Torque Value:** 230 N•m \[ 170 ft-lb \]
- Step 3 **Torque Value:** 346 N•m \[ 255 ft-lb \]
- Step 4 Loosen Completely
- Step 5 Repeat Steps 1 through 3

For engines built on or after January 5, 1998, beginning with engine serial number first 11875596, use this procedure that includes a new 3/4 inch diameter, grade 12.9 capscrew, Part Number [[3411337]].

![[mb8cvoa.png]]

Coat the capscrew threads and both sides of the washers with clean lubricating engine oil. Drain the excess oil from the capscrews before installing them in the cylinder block. This allows the highest possible preload from the input torque.

Tighten the main bearing capscrews to the following torque values:

- Step 1 **Torque Value:** 69 N•m \[ 50 ft-lb \]
- Step 2 **Torque Value:** 136 N•m \[ 100 ft-lb \]
- Step 3 **Torque Value:** 190 N•m \[ 140 ft-lb \]
- Step 4 Rotate the main bearing capscrew 90 degrees in the direction of tightening while following the instructions below:
- Step 5 Loosen the main bearing capscrews completely
- Step 6 Repeat Steps 1 through 4

**Line Bore GuidelinesM11/ISM Guidelines**

If one or more of the main bearing bores has a clearance problem (less than 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \], repair of the engine requires line boring of the main bearing bore in the cylinder block. Refer to the L10/M11 Alternative Repair Manual, Bulletin 3810310-01, for the line-boring procedure.

**N14 Guidelines**

When it has been determined that one or more of the main bores has a clearance problem (less than 0.051 mm \[ 0.002 in \]), repair of the engine requires line boring of the cylinder block described below. Refer to Section ARM (001-020) in the Alternative Repair Manual, Bulletin 3379035.

Remove the main bearings from all journals

![[mb2cahf.png]]

Install two of the original main bearing caps that have no fretting with the original grade 8 capscrews.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> It is recommended to go this in two journals that are far apart from each other (number 1 and number 7 are ideal). These journals are your reference journals.

![[mb2cvsa.png]]

Install the remaining main bearing caps with new grade 12.9 capscrews, Part Number [[3411337]].

Tighten as follows:

- Step 1 **Torque Value:** 68 N•m \[ 50 ft-lb \]
- Step 2 **Torque Value:** 136 N•m \[ 100 ft-lb \]
- Step 3 **Torque Value:** 190 N•m \[ 140 ft-lb \]
- Step 4 Tighten 90 degrees

![[mb2brws.png]]

Use a dial indicator to align the boring bar with the centers of the journals that have the original grade 8 capscrews.

Line-bore the journals that have the grade 12.9 capscrews.

![[mb2cvsa.png]]

Replace the grade 8 capscrews in the two reference journals with grade 12.9 capscrews and tighten in accordance with the procedure outlined above.

![[mb2brws.png]]

Use a dial indicator to align the boring bar with the centers of two of the newly bored journals.

Line-bore the two reference journals.

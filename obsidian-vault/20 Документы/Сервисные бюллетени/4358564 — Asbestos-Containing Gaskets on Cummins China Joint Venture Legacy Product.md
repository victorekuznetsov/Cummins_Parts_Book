## Asbestos-Containing Gaskets on Cummins China Joint Venture Legacy Product

## Introduction

This document outlines a population of legacy engines produced at China Joint Venture engine plants that may have asbestos-containing gaskets. There are a variety of gaskets that have been identified as being at risk. The populations of affected engines are outlined below, along with detailed information about the location and handling of these gaskets.

Engines with gaskets that may contain asbestos should be marked with an “A” (decal Part Number 4326238) to inform the technicians servicing these engines of the possible asbestos-containing gaskets. This document serves as an additional callout of affected engines to provide awareness and safety for individuals servicing the affected engine population.

Safe work procedures for gaskets have been outlined in Safe Work Procedure for Internal Engine and Engine Component Gaskets, Service Bulletin 432604, and Cummins Virtual College course number 1192. Below is an excerpt from the service bulletin that provides further explanation around this issue.

**“Rationale:** Diesel engines and components contain various types of gaskets. Many engine and component gasket materials are non-fibrous rubber, metal, or silicone; however, some are fibrous and some original equipment manufacturer (OEM), as well as third-party, utilized or owner installed fibrous gaskets may have been manufactured with an asbestos ingredient. Nevertheless, statistical analysis of analogous Cummins® commissioned personal breathing zone airborne asbestos fiber exposure assessments show potential exposures to airborne asbestos fibers are well controlled when good work practices and procedures are used when handling or removing asbestos-containing gaskets.

**Note 1:** In many countries around the world, gaskets manufactured with an asbestos ingredient (chrysotile) are readily available from third-party vendors, are **not** a regulated product, and may find their way into use on a Cummins® engine.

**Note 2:** Fibrous looking gaskets that may contain asbestos are **not** able to be confirmed without analytical testing. Testing a gasket to determine its composition is difficult, if **not** impossible, without first disassembling the engine or engine component. However, by following the guidance provided by the Internal Engine and Engine Component Gaskets SWP – Service Centers, the potential for airborne fiber release is minimal."

## Section 1: Chongqing Cummins® Engine Company (CCEC) PT™ Fuel Pump Gasket Care

It has come to the attention of Cummins Inc. that some of the gaskets originally supplied in PT™ Fuel Pumps sourced from China and used in CCEC produced engines (L10, LTA10, M11, NT/NTA 855, KT/KTA/KTTA19, K/KT/KTA 38, KTA50), prior to 31 July 2011, contain some percentage of asbestos. The plant of origin and the manufacturing date of the engine can be obtained from the Engine Serial Number dataplate. Fuel pump serial numbers after 337484 are **not** subject to this notice. Cummins Inc, believes that the encapsulated chrysotile asbestos material present in these gaskets does **not** release asbestos fibers that can be inhaled, and therefore, does **not** present an increased safety risk unless it is handled in such a manner as to abrade, or disturb the integrity of the gasket surface, which could occur during some servicing. We do **not** believe that normal servicing of the PT™ Fuel Pumps sourced from China will cause this to occur. Cummins Inc, has conducted tests which have confirmed our belief that these gaskets can be safely handled and removed. The normal operation of these units does **not** require you to take any additional precautions.

A sample of a typical PT™ fuel pump data tag, from a China sourced PT™ fuel pump, is included below for reference. The fuel pump serial number is the six digit number in the top right corner of the dataplate. The fuel pump with this dataplate was manufactured after the changeover to non-asbestos material.

## Section 3: Chongqing Cummins® Engine Company (CCEC) Base Engine Gasket Care

It has come to the attention of Cummins Inc. that some of the gaskets originally supplied for use in CCEC produced engines (L10, LTA10, M11, NT/NTA 855, KT/KTA/KTTA19, K/KT/KTA 38, KTA50) prior to 31 July 31 2006, contain some percentage of asbestos. The plant of origin and the manufacturing date of the engine can be obtained from the Engine Serial Number dataplate. Cummins Inc, believes that the encapsulated chrysotile asbestos material present in these gaskets does **not** release asbestos fibers that can be inhaled, and therefore does **not** present an increased safety risk unless it is handled in such a manner as to abrade, or disturb the integrity of the gasket surface, which could occur during some servicing. We do **not** believe that normal servicing of the engine will cause this to occur. Cummins Inc, has conducted tests which have confirmed our belief that these gaskets can be safely handled and removed. The normal operation of these units does **not** require you to take any additional precautions.

The gaskets that potentially contain asbestos include but are **not** limited to cylinder head gaskets, exhaust manifold gaskets, and turbocharger manifold gaskets. The asbestos-containing gaskets were phased out of use over time, so each engine may have a varying number of gaskets that contain asbestos. Gaskets used in any engine built on or after 31 July 2006 do **not** contain asbestos.

Any used gasket(s) from these units should be disposed of by following all local rules and regulations regarding their disposal.

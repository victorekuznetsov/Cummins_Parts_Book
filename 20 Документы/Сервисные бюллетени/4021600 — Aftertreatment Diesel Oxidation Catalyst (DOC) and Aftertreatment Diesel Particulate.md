## I. Introduction

- This document provides reuse guidelines for the aftertreatment diesel oxidation catalyst (DOC) and aftertreatment diesel particulate filter (DPF), in conjunction with the information provided within the procedures listed in Table 29 below. See the corresponding Service Manual.
- The reuse guidelines contained in this document are the **only** Cummins Inc. approved inspection methods and pass/fail criteria with regards to inspecting an aftertreatment DPF for reuse. Many aftertreatment DPF cleaning machine manufacturers provide pass/fail criteria for aftertreatment DPFs. Pass/fail criteria based on flow-through resistance characteristics do **not** align with Cummins® aftertreatment DPF reuse guidelines found in this bulletin and are **not** to be used to determine if a Cummins® aftertreatment DPF is acceptable for reuse.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> The Euro VI product operates an exchange program for the DPF in the event where the maintenance interval is reached. The filter will undergo a core acceptance inspection following the guidelines set out in the ReCon® handbook. See link provided.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> Please refer to the “Filter Modules – Particulate – Other – 2010 and Future MR/HD” section in the ReCon® Core Acceptance Handbook (https://quickserve.cummins.com/qs3/portal/service/manual/en/4360362/)

**VI. Aftertreatment Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) Inspection**

An internally cracked or melted aftertreatment DPF can show damage in three ways:

1. Aftertreatment DPF outlet channels have soot stains
2. Exhaust system outlet has excessive soot buildup
3. Black smoke during engine operation

Repair Direction:

- If DPF outlet channels have soot stains (1); replace DPF.
- If exhaust system outlet has excessive soot buildup (2) and there is black smoke during engine operation (3); replace DPF. The source of excessive soot that caused the cracking or melting **must** be investigated.

Additional Information:

There are two reasons aftertreatment DPF outlet channels would **not** have soot staining, but unit would have excessive soot buildup on exhaust outlet and black smoke during engine operation:

1. If an active regeneration was performed shortly before the DPF was removed and inspected, this could have removed the soot from the channels of the DPF outlet.
2. If the cracking or melting occurs close to the DPF inlet face, the soot will **not** build-up in the outlet channel wall and will **not** be visible. See Figure 30 below.

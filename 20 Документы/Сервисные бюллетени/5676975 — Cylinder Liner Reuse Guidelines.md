## Cylinder Liner Reuse Guidelines

**Introduction**

This document provides reuse guidelines for cylinder liners and supplements the information provided in the Service Manuals listed in Table 1 below. These guidelines are to be consulted when determining if cylinder liners are acceptable for reuse.

- ISX12N CM2380 X12B
- L9N CM2380 L124B/L9N CM2380 L130B

**Diagnostic Procedures**

Do **not** remove the cylinder head without first performing a cylinder compression test. See corresponding Service Manual. Reference Procedure 014-012 in Section 14.

Visual inspection of a cylinder liner alone should **not** result in replacement. Visual defects **must** also correlate with a physical defect or out of specification measurement. For example, a failed visual inspection must be paired with:

- A defect that can be caught with a fingernail
- An out of specification compression test
- Cylinder liner reuse measurements out of specification

Cylinder liners **must** be clean and dry for an accurate inspection. Presence of oil, coolant, and other debris or contaminants from cylinder head removal may cause misinterpretation of cylinder liner condition. See the Clean and Inspect for Reuse section of the corresponding Service Manual. Reference Procedure 001-028 in Section 1.

Modern cylinder liner finishes may falsely appear as moderately polished or heavily polished, even when there is no wear present, depending on angle of the light source and reflections. When inspecting cylinder liners, move a focused light source around multiple locations above and in the cylinder liner to check for presence of cross hatching in all locations. It is **not** possible to inspect all areas of an installed cylinder liner with a single fixed light source. Most angles of light source produce a reflection that may give a false appearance of cylinder liner polishing across large regions of the cylinder liner. This makes photographing the cross hatching of a liner difficult. For this reason, cross hatching is **only** visible in Figures 1 through 9 across some regions of the reusable cylinder liners shown in this document.

Scratches, discoloration, and/or imperfections that are **not** able to be felt with a fingernail do **not** impact oil consumption or compression and do **not** warrant cylinder liner replacement.

**Definitions**

Moderate Polish

- A moderate polish produces a bright mirror finish in the worn area with traces of the original hone marks or an indication of an etch pattern. Moderate polishing appearance alone does **not** warrant cylinder liner replacement.

Heavy Polish

- A heavy polish produces a bright mirror finish in the worn area with **no** traces of hone marks or an etch pattern regardless of what angle the light is. Heavy polish **must** be present around a minimum percentage of the bore circumference and minimum percentage of the piston ring travel area to warrant cylinder liner replacement. See the Clean and Inspect for Reuse section of the corresponding Service Manual. Reference Procedure 001-028 Cylinder Liner in Section 1.
- Heavy polish is normally an indication of debris ingestion also known as “dusting”. Dusting is a non-warrantable failure mode.

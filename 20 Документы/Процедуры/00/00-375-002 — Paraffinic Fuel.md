### General Information

Petroleum diesel contains chemical compounds known as paraffins and aromatics. Paraffinic fuels are predominantly paraffins and are derived from a variety of sources. The most common sources are natural gas, coal, plant oils, and animal fats. Natural gas fuels are often referred to as gas- to-liquid (GTL). Fuels from plant oils and fats are often referred to as renewable diesel (RD) or hydrogenated vegetable oil (HVO). Renewable diesels are **not** the same as biodiesel or fatty acid methyl ester (FAME) which are chemically different and have different properties. Unique characteristics of paraffin-based fuel include reduced density, limited sulfur/aromatics content, higher hydrogen to carbon (H/C) ratio, and increased cetane. Generally, paraffinic fuels have a density about 7 percent lower than petroleum diesels and can result up to in about 5 percent lower energy content by volume, resulting in reduced fuel economy and possible reduction in engine power.

Cummins Inc. testing indicates that paraffinic fuels generally result in lower smoke emissions, reduced power output, and decreased fuel economy. The European specification for paraffinic fuels is defined in EN 15940. Paraffinic fuels **must** meet the requirements of either Table 8 of this section or EN 15940. The requirements of EN 15940 **must** be achieved. The minimum requirement is to meet Section 1: Diesel Fuel (00-375-001), excluding the density requirement in Table 1, Cummins Required Fuel Properties. These fuels are approved for use in the engines listed in Table 9: Products and Applications Approved for up to 100% Paraffinic Fuel Blends.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> For all other Cummins® products that are **not** listed in Table 9 are **not** approved to 100 percent blends, the Paraffinic component fuel is allowed in any blend proportions provided that the final blended diesel fuel complies with EN 590 or the requirements listed in Table 1 in this bulletin for all Cummins® engines.

The user is responsible to verify/obtain the proper local, regional, or national exemptions required for the use of paraffinic fuels in any emissions regulated Cummins® engine.

Paraffinic fuels like conventional diesel fuel can be affected by environmental factors such as cold temperatures that can inhibit proper operation of Cummins engines®. The user is responsible for procuring paraffinic fuel that conforms to the Cloud Point requirements in Table 8 and based temperature factors in the region of use.

Warranty and the Use of Paraffinic Fuel in Cummins® Engines

Cummins® Engine Warranty covers malfunctions that are a result of defects in material or factory workmanship. Engine damage, service issues, and/or performance issues determined by Cummins® to be caused by using paraffinic fuel **not** meeting the specifications outlined in this document are **not** considered to be defects in material or workmanship and are **not** covered under Cummins® engine warranty.

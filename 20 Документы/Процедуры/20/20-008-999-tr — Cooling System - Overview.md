with Electronically Actuated Injector

The QSK19 CM850 engine maintains the standard 1-pump, 1-loop engine cooling system that requires both a radiator and air-to-air charge air cooler.

Heat rejection to coolant has been slightly reduced as a result of lower intake air temperatures and increased air flow. Maximum top tank temperature, however, remains unchanged at 212 °F \[100 °C\]. The torque converter cooler option remains the same and the corrosion filter continues to be required and also remains unchanged. The service interval of the corrosion filter is 250 hours.

The coolant inlet and outlet connections are the same size and are located in the same location as the Tier 2, QSK19 engine configuration. As such, the same water inlet and water outlet options will be offered.

The turbocharged QSK19 Tier 3 engine requires the use of an air-to-air heat exchanger to reduce the temperature of the air entering the intake manifold. This type of heat exchanger is termed an air-to-air charge air cooler (CAC). The charge air cooler **must** be designed to meet the new intake manifold temperature target of 49 °C \[120 °F\] on a 25 °C \[77 °F\] ambient day.

The charge air cooling system includes the ducting from the turbocharger compressor outlet to the charge air cooler air inlet and from the charge air cooler air outlet to the engine intake manifold. The charge air cooler core and plumbing **must** be designed to meet the temperature and pressure requirements specified in the appropriate data sheets. It **must** also be resistant to corrosion and vibration (See AEB 24.06.).

Rail Applications

Because the QSK19 Rail engine uses an air-to-air aftercooler, there is **no** aftercooler element in the intake manifold that uses coolant to cool the intake air. The thermostat housing has been modified to allow for coolant to flow to the OEM supplied radiator. The thermostat housing also contains a plug for the coolant bypass port. The plug is held in place by a tube clamp.

> [!note] Note · Примечание
> The thermostat housing does **not** have thermostats. The coolant temperatures is controlled by the OEM supplied thermostats.

Marine Applications

Heat Exchanger

The marine heat exchanger assembly is attached to the expansion tank

The marine heat exchanger is a plate or tube, tube design and is located on the front side of the engine. The plates are removable for cleaning. A sacrificial plug is located in the front cavity of the heat exchanger sea water passage.

The engine coolant flows from the thermostat housing outlet into the top rear of the heat exchanger and is directed over the plates or tubes. The engine coolant exits the cooler at the bottom rear of the heat exchanger. The engine coolant is then returned to the engine coolant pump inlet via a transfer tube.

The cooling sea water enters the bottom rear of the heat exchanger. The sea water flows through the tubes to the front, nondivided cavity. The sea water then flows through the tubes or plates and is discharged out of the top side of the heat exchanger. The sea water is then discharged overboard.

![[nobox.png]]

Marine Keel Cooling

The marine single loop LTA cooling flow and engine temperature is controlled with one thermostat located in the thermostat housing. The engine coolant flows to the vessel mounted keel coolers through flexible couplings located at the front of the engine. The water pump inlet and outlet connections have been designed with a second connection for emergency inlet and outlet coolant connections.

The thermostat housing contains diagnostic ports for troubleshooting the keel coolers and after cooler.
